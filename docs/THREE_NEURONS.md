# Experiment B — three biophysical neurons

Unlike Experiment A (the full connectome graph), this is an **independent** detailed-membrane demonstration with only three cells.

Each neuron follows the classical **Hodgkin–Huxley sodium/potassium ion-channel differential equations**: membrane capacitance, voltage-sensitive gating variables m/h/n, ionic conductances, reversal potentials, and action potentials generated by those equations. The cells also communicate through simple conductance-based chemical synapses: cell 0 excites cells 1 and 2; cell 2 inhibits cell 1.

**Important:** The original classical HH parameters were derived from squid axon experiments, **not** measurements from the fruit fly. The synaptic weights and motif here are illustrative, not reconstructed fly physiology. These are realistic principles of neural electrophysiology, not a specific drosophila microcircuit.

## Windows (double click or terminal)

Double-click **`Run-Three-Neurons.cmd`** from the repository root. It creates a Python virtual environment, installs dependencies, runs the experiment, and produces:

- `results/biophysical/hh_neurons.csv` — time-indexed voltages and detected spikes for all three cells.
- `results/biophysical/hh_neurons.png` — a chart showing their membrane voltage traces.
- `results/biophysical/summary.json` — spike counts.

Or run manually with Python 3.11+:

```powershell
py -3 -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -e ".[plots]"
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m fruit_fly_stuff.biophysical --plot
```

Try the **no-connections control**:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m fruit_fly_stuff.biophysical --no-synapses --plot --output results/no-synapses
```

Compare plots: when synapses are disabled, the two unstimulated cells should remain near their resting potential rather than responding to cell 0.

## What this does not simulate

The three neurons are **single-compartment** rather than branching multicompartment neurons. It does not model FlyWire anatomy, fly-specific channel kinetics, receptor types, dendritic propagation, plasticity, or behavior. It is intended as a controlled starting point for testing biophysical modeling and for comparing models at different levels of detail.
